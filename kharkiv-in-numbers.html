How big is Kharkiv?

The numbers below show how large Kharkiv is and how this size affects everyday life. Some of them are easier to understand when you compare them with a city you already know. The numbers also show that the city was developed as a major centre, with universities, a metro system and a lot of industry.

Key numbers

  • 1.4M population
  • 2 largest city in Ukraine
  • 1654 year founded
  • 350 km² city area
  • 300k students
  • 38 higher education institutions

Kharkiv compared with Amsterdam

More Than Just Numbers

Kharkiv is a very large city with around 1.4 million people and an area of about 350 km². To compare, Amsterdam covers around 219 km², so Kharkiv is about 1.6 times bigger by area. On paper, these are just numbers, but when you live there, you really feel the size of the city. If your university, work or friends are in another part of Kharkiv, you can spend a lot of time just getting there.

Different districts can also feel very different from each other, with their own parks, shops and places where people spend their time. You can live in Kharkiv for years and still have parts of the city where you almost never go.

A city of students

Kharkiv is not only big in size — it was also built as an important centre of education, science and transport. The city has 38 higher education institutions, including 13 national universities, with around 300,000 students and 17,000 teachers and researchers. Around 12,000 of these students came from other countries, so international students were a normal part of life in Kharkiv.

To understand the scale, 300,000 students is around one third of the population of Amsterdam, which means that about one in five people in Kharkiv was a student.

Metro

The city also has its own metro, opened in 1975 as the second metro system in Ukraine, with three lines and 30 stations. All of this shows that Kharkiv was developed not just as a large city, but as a place where people came to study, work and do research.

Founded in 1654

Kharkiv was founded in 1654 as a Cossack fortress, which means the city is more than 370 years old. But when you see its wide streets, large Soviet buildings and newer districts, Kharkiv can look much younger than it really is. This can be surprising for someone who is learning about the city for the first time.
